ABSTRACT
Embodied agents benefit from 3D environments that combine visual fidelity to
real-world observations with physical interactivity. Existing single-image tabletop
reconstruction methods recover plausible scene geometry but typically represent
objects as monolithic rigid bodies, limiting interaction to whole-object rigid mo-
tion and precluding executable part-level articulation. Meanwhile, recovering a
scene layout consistent with the input view remains challenging because a single
observation may admit multiple plausible pose–scale configurations. We present
ArticuTable, a single-image 3D tabletop reconstruction framework that recov-
ers both executable part-level articulation and an input-view-consistent scene lay-
out. For object modeling, we introduce generation-robust articulation modeling
(GRAM), which combines joint fitting guided by a multimodal large language
model with semantic state reasoning to recover reliable joint parameters and valid
motion ranges from imperfect monolithic proxy meshes, thereby converting them
into executable articulated assets. For scene layout, we introduce progressive
semantic-geometric scene registration (PSGSR), which progressively narrows the
pose–scale search space under complementary metric, planar, and input-view con-
straints and resolves orientation ambiguity through structure-aware semantic cor-
respondences, yielding a scene layout consistent with the input view. We further

INTRODUCTION
Rapid advances in embodied intelligence have increased demand for large-scale collections of 3D
scenes. Such scenes should faithfully reproduce object appearance and spatial layout while encod-
ing the physical and kinematic properties required for interaction (Yang et al., 2024; Nasiriany et al.,
2024; Lee et al., 2025; Pfaff et al., 2025; Xia et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026a). Tabletop scenes are par-
ticularly important because they concentrate diverse object instances and dense spatial relationships
within compact workspaces, making them natural testbeds for fine-grained manipulation (Wang
et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2026). However, manually authoring visually faithful and physically inter-
active tabletop scenes is labor-intensive and difficult to scale. Automatically reconstructing such
scenes from readily available monocular RGB images therefore offers a practical and scalable al-
ternative. Recent single-image 3D scene reconstruction methods have made substantial progress
in decomposing scenes into object instances, reconstructing per-instance geometry, and recovering
their spatial arrangements (Huang et al., 2025; Yao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026).
A prevalent paradigm in these works is instance-wise reconstruction and registration (G¨umeli et al.,
2022; Yao et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026). Under this paradigm, each object is independently re-
constructed or retrieved as a 3D asset and then registered to a shared scene coordinate frame by
estimating its pose and scale from the input view. Although this factorization provides detailed
object geometry and appearance, the resulting assets typically remain monolithic surface meshes.
Consequently, reconstructed scenes support object-level rigid-body interaction but lack physically
meaningful part-level motion. A natural solution is to apply existing articulation modeling methods
to the reconstructed assets. However, many existing methods are trained and quantitatively evaluated
on curated articulated-object datasets whose assets have well-defined part structures and articulation
annotations (Xiang et al., 2020; Li et al., 2026b). Image-generated proxy meshes differ substan-
tially: part boundaries may be fused, contact geometry may be distorted, and internal structures may
be incomplete. These artifacts can make joint parameters estimated from local geometry unreliable
and cause motion ranges inferred from learned priors or geometric constraints to yield implausible
articulated states. Consequently, directly applying existing articulation modeling methods to gener-
ated meshes may fail to produce reliable executable articulated assets. Scene registration presents
a separate challenge. A single input view may admit multiple plausible pose–scale configurations,
some of which attain comparable alignment scores. Existing methods commonly recover scene lay-
outs by directly optimizing object poses and scales against the input view, resulting in a non-convex
objective with competing basins of attraction. The solution therefore depends strongly on initial-
ization, and an unfavorable initial estimate may lead local refinement to a geometrically incorrect
arrangement that is inconsistent with the input view.
To address these challenges, we present ArticuTable, a framework for reconstructing simulation-
ready tabletop scenes containing both rigid and articulated objects from a single RGB image. As
illustrated in Fig. 1, ArticuTable preserves the observed instance arrangement while enabling exe-
cutable part-level articulation; the overall framework is shown in Fig. 2. For object modeling, we
introduce generation-robust articulation modeling (GRAM). GRAM first decomposes a monolithic
proxy mesh into geometric primitives and organizes them into functional links and a rooted kine-
matic tree. To make joint estimation robust to distorted proxy geometry, MLLM-guided joint fitting
first constructs joint-type-specific geometric hypotheses and then uses a multimodal large language
model (MLLM) with global multimodal and structural evidence to select or correct joint axes and
pivots. Meanwhile, semantic state reasoning combines functional semantic evidence with geometric
feasibility to infer valid motion ranges, reducing its reliance on geometric or collision constraints
derived from imperfect meshes. The resulting kinematic structure, joint parameters, and motion
ranges are exported as an executable URDF asset. For scene layout recovery, we introduce progres-
sive semantic-geometric scene registration (PSGSR). Its progressive multi-constraint registration
(PMCR) progressively restricts the pose–scale search space: point-cloud alignment first provides a
coarse metric estimate, top-view registration then refines the planar layout, and input-view regis-
tration finally improves consistency with the original observation. Each stage is initialized by the
preceding estimate, reducing the risk that subsequent local refinement enters an incorrect basin of
2


attraction. Structure-aware semantic pose selection (SASPS) ranks yaw candidates in both stages us-
ing structure-weighted bidirectional semantic correspondences and initializes differentiable refine-
ment with the selected pose. Efficient 2D top-view rotations reduce input-view search to residual
symmetric hypotheses, enabling PSGSR to recover an input-view-consistent layout.
We further construct ArticuTable-100, a curated collection of 100 simulation-ready tabletop scenes
containing visual meshes, collision geometry, and executable articulated assets. Quantitative com-
parisons, simulation-based evaluation, and a user study assess the reconstructed scenes in terms of
visual fidelity, input-view consistency, articulation quality, and physical plausibility.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We present ArticuTable, a framework that reconstructs simulation-ready tabletop scenes
from a single RGB image while simultaneously enabling executable part-level articulation
and preserving scene-layout consistency with the input view.
• We introduce GRAM, which combines MLLM-guided joint fitting with semantic state
reasoning to address unreliable contact-derived joint estimates and mesh-induced motion-
range errors, enabling executable URDF asset generation from imperfect monolithic
meshes without articulation-specific training or fine-tuning.
• We introduce PSGSR, which integrates progressive geometric and visual constraints with
structure-aware semantic pose reasoning to resolve pose ambiguity and progressively re-
cover an input-view-consistent scene layout.
• We contribute ArticuTable-100, a curated collection of 100 simulation-ready tabletop
scenes containing visual meshes, collision geometry, and executable articulated assets.

RELATED WORK
Single-Image 3D Scene Reconstruction. Single-image 3D scene reconstruction has progressed
from holistic indoor modeling toward object-centric scene assembly for embodied interaction. To-
tal3DUnderstanding (Nie et al., 2020) jointly estimates room layout, camera and object poses, and
object meshes, while Text2Room (H¨ollein et al., 2023) synthesizes textured room-scale geometry.
Feed-forward methods such as DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024) and VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) directly
estimate camera parameters and dense geometry. For robotic applications, Digital Cousins (Dai
et al., 2025) constructs interactive scene variations that preserve geometric and semantic affordances.
More closely related to our tabletop setting, TabletopGen (Wang et al., 2026) generates per-object
assets and assembles collision-free scenes through differentiable rotation optimization and top-view
spatial alignment.
Foundation Models for Object-Centric Reconstruction. Foundation models connect semantic
interpretation with geometric synthesis in object-centric reconstruction. Large multimodal models
reason about object functions and spatial relationships (OpenAI, 2025; Liu et al., 2023a), while
language priors support compositional scene-layout generation (Feng et al., 2023). Promptable seg-
mentation grounds these cues into object regions in images (Kirillov et al., 2023; Carion et al., 2026)
and parts in native 3D geometry (Ma et al., 2025). Image-conditioned generators then lift isolated
object observations into detailed open-domain 3D assets (Team, 2025; Xiang et al., 2025; 2026).
Articulated Object Modeling. For interactive tabletop scenes, reconstructed static geometry must
be endowed with part structures, kinematic relationships, and joint constraints. Given observations
before and after interaction or across articulation states, motion can be inferred from geometric
changes (Jiang et al., 2022b; Liu et al., 2023b). Image-based methods use learned priors, retrieval,
or vision-language reasoning (Jiang et al., 2022a; Chen et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Zhao et al.,
2025; Le et al., 2025). Static-shape methods use learned prediction (Wang et al., 2019; Yan et al.,
2020; Goyal et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026b), vision-language reasoning (Qiu et al., 2025), or geometric
and physical optimization (Xu et al., 2026).
Despite these advances, reconstructing a tabletop scene with both executable part-level articulation
and single-view consistency remains challenging. Generated-mesh artifacts can corrupt joint esti-
mation and motion-range inference, while dense arrangements and occlusions hinder input-view-
consistent layout recovery. ArticuTable addresses these challenges through GRAM for executable
articulated asset generation and PSGSR for progressive scene registration and pose disambiguation.

placements of operable proxies are transferred to their URDF counterparts for scene assembly.
3.2
INSTANCE-AWARE 3D ASSET RECONSTRUCTION
Semantic-first pipelines may omit objects because only previously recognized categories are passed
to instance segmentation. We therefore decouple instance discovery from semantic recognition.
Given the input image and a synthesized top-view hypothesis, a class-agnostic segmenter indepen-
dently extracts anonymous instance proposals in both views, whose identifiers carry no category
information. Front-view proposals are associated with top-view proposals through one-to-one ge-
ometric matching based on their table-normalized horizontal extents, relative spatial positions, and
4


mask areas. An MLLM then identifies reconstructable scene instances, assigns each one a semantic
identity and operability type, and estimates object dimensions, placements, and direct support rela-
tions from the paired scene observations. For each identified instance, we generate a reconstruction-
ready condition image that preserves its appearance, overall pose, and articulation state, while using
the matched top view only as auxiliary evidence for occluded geometry. An image-conditioned 3D
generator then reconstructs an independent proxy mesh, which is standardized to the gravity-aligned
coordinate frame used by GRAM and PSGSR.

Progressive Multi-Constraint Registration.
The supporting table first establishes a shared metric

alignment provides a coarse object placement using a trimmed nearest-surface distance. Top-view
registration then refines the planar position, footprint scale, and yaw under the synthesized top-view

object-to-object support relations. By propagating each intermediate estimate to the subsequent
stage, PMCR progressively introduces metric, planar, and input-view constraints, narrowing the
placement search space before each refinement.

3.5
CONSTRUCTION OF ARTICUTABLE-100
We construct ArticuTable-100, a manually curated collection of 100 simulation-ready tabletop
scenes reconstructed by ArticuTable. Candidate reconstructions are evaluated for visual fidelity,
physical validity, and articulation executability. The resulting collection spans diverse tabletop ar-
rangements, object combinations, and articulated object types. Each scene is distributed as a self-
contained USDZ package directly loadable in Isaac Sim, containing textured visual assets, collision
geometry for simulation, and executable articulated object models.

EXPERIMENTS
4.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
We evaluate scene reconstruction and registration on 150 generated tabletop reference images
against ACDC, Gen3DSR, MIDI, and TabletopGen (Dai et al., 2025; Ardelean et al., 2025; Huang
et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026). All baselines share inputs and views; where applicable, they use

evaluated on all 243 Lightwheel assets (Li et al., 2026b) following the official protocol, covering
part decomposition, articulated geometry, and joint estimation. ArticuTable uses fixed pretrained
models without task-specific training or fine-tuning. Complete baseline settings, metric definitions,
implementation details, and ablation protocols are provided in Appendix D.

CONCLUSION
We presented ArticuTable, a framework for reconstructing simulation-ready tabletop scenes con-
taining both rigid and articulated objects from a single RGB image. GRAM transforms monolithic
object meshes into executable articulated assets by recovering functional links, rooted kinematic
structures, and joint parameters. PSGSR improves scene registration through progressively con-
strained metric, planar, and input-view stages, while SASPS selects reliable yaw hypotheses for dif-
ferentiable refinement. Experiments demonstrate improvements in scene-level visual consistency,
physical validity, articulation quality, and joint estimation. We further introduced ArticuTable-100,
a curated collection of 100 simulation-ready scenes for downstream embodied manipulation. To-
gether, these contributions provide a unified basis for constructing visually faithful and physically
interactive tabletop environments from monocular observations.

A.3
INSTANCE UNDERSTANDING AND OBJECT-WISE RECONSTRUCTION
Semantic reasoning is performed after anonymous proposals have been discovered and associated.
An MLLM examines each input-view proposal, determines whether it represents a complete and
independently reconstructable scene instance, and assigns each retained instance a semantic identity
and operability type. At the scene level, the MLLM further estimates object dimensions, planar
placements, orientations, and direct support relations from the input and top-view observations,
producing a support hierarchy rooted at the table. When a cross-view match is available, the corre-
sponding top-view observation provides complementary evidence for object footprint and occluded
geometry without overriding the appearance or physical state observed in the input image.
For every retained instance, we construct a target-specific condition image in which the input obser-
vation provides the primary evidence for the object’s appearance, overall pose, and articulation state,
while the matched top view is used only to complete occluded geometry. TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al.,

2026) reconstructs each condition image as an independent proxy mesh. The reconstructed meshes
are then centered and gravity-aligned, while the inferred support hierarchy is passed to PSGSR for
scene-level placement.

The MLLM uses the paired views, primitive mesh, source and primary images, and boundary evi-

spin. Each link is materialized as a textured visual mesh and a convex-hull collision mesh.
B.2
MLLM-GUIDED JOINT FITTING
For each joint j = (Lp, Lc), GRAM approximates the contact region using child surface vertices
whose nearest parent surface lies within a scale-adaptive distance, preferring boundary vertices when
available. The complete subtree rooted at Lc is treated as the moving body, while all links outside
that subtree form the obstacle set. Consequently, joint fitting and collision evaluation follow the
predicted kinematic hierarchy rather than moving the child link in isolation.
For a hinge joint, the contact centroid initializes the pivot and the largest-variance principal direction
of the contact set initializes the axis. For a spin joint, GRAM estimates the contact plane by PCA, fits
a circle in that plane using seeded RANSAC (Fischler & Bolles, 1981) followed by least squares,
and lifts the circle center and plane normal back to 3D. For a prismatic joint, PCA of the child
vertices yields three candidate axes. The MLLM selects one candidate and its positive direction
using the paired renderings and the source image. For revolute joints, the MLLM evaluates the
contact-derived axis line using the paired views, source image, link geometry, and global kinematic
structure. It corrects the axis direction when supported by explicit visual or mechanical evidence
and estimates the pivot location from the visible attachment and rotation structure. When sufficient
directional evidence is unavailable, the contact-derived axis direction is retained.
B.3
SEMANTIC STATE REASONING AND INTRINSIC LIMITS
Joint geometry alone does not identify which configuration of an image-generated mesh should be
treated as the semantic zero. GRAM therefore separates geometric closure recovery from semantic
extent reasoning. For hinge joints, connected planar patches on the parent and child links generate
candidate closed configurations around the fitted axis, while the complete moving subtree partic-
ipates in geometric and collision-based validation. Candidates are ranked using plane alignment,
overlap, axis proximity, and geometric consistency, yielding a plane-closed configuration and the
signed displacement of the reconstructed mesh from that configuration. For spin joints, GRAM
15


searches for the nearest closed configuration in both rotational directions while moving the com-
plete child subtree. Axis-local surfaces within an adaptive neighborhood of the rotation axis are
excluded from the collision predicate, preventing axle-like contact from blocking an otherwise valid
rotation.
Prismatic joints use their selected outward direction to define a nonnegative coordinate from the
canonical closed state. Motion is scanned in both directions until collision or loss of parent contact;
a displacement is considered derailed when fewer than 5% of the transformed contact samples re-
main close to the parent. These prismatic scans provide physical fallback intervals and geometric
feasibility evidence.

D.3
MANUAL AUDIT OF GENERATED ARTICULATIONS
Evaluation setup.
We conduct an expert manual audit over the complete 150-scene evaluation
set to assess articulation quality on the imperfect image-generated meshes encountered by the full
reconstruction pipeline. A primary expert evaluates all candidate assets, and a second expert in-
dependently re-evaluates a uniformly random 20% sample. Among the 150 scenes, 149 contain at
least one candidate articulated asset, while one contains no movable object and therefore requires
no joint-level assessment. The audit covers all 425 candidate assets and 527 predicted joints.
Rating protocol.
For each predicted joint, the evaluator inspects the input object crop, recon-
structed initial state, and motion sweep, and separately scores structure and motion on a three-level
scale. The structure score evaluates functional link grouping, joint type, axis, and pivot plausibility,
while the motion score evaluates whether the predicted motion range remains functionally plausible
without severe interpenetration, detachment, or geometric degeneration. A joint passes when both
scores are 2, is partial when neither score is 0 and at least one score is 1, and fails when either score
is 0. For multi-joint objects, the asset-level result is the worst result among its joints.

D.4
IMPLEMENTATION AND ABLATION PROTOCOLS
We use GPT-5 (OpenAI, 2025) for multimodal scene and object analysis, auxiliary-view generation,
and the multimodal kinematic reasoning performed within GRAM. SAM 3 (Carion et al., 2026)
provides class-agnostic instance segmentation, TRELLIS.2 (Xiang et al., 2026) performs object-
wise 3D reconstruction, and P3-SAM (Ma et al., 2025) provides primitive segmentation for GRAM.
VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) estimates dense point maps and camera parameters, while SASPS uses a
frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 encoder. All pretrained components are used without task-specific training
or fine-tuning.
Scene assembly and rendering are performed in Blender. Differentiable rasterization in PSGSR is
implemented with nvdiffrast (Laine et al., 2020). Geometric collision checks within GRAM and
scene-conditioned motion-range analysis use Trimesh/FCL (Pan et al., 2012), while simulation-
based physical evaluation is performed in Isaac Sim with PhysX.
All PSGSR variants share the same input images, proxy assets, and camera parameters, differing
only in the evaluated registration components. GRAM component ablations follow a 2 × 2 factorial
design over MLLM-guided joint fitting and semantic state reasoning. The marginal effect of each
component is computed by averaging over the two settings of the other component.
Point-cloud alignment is optimized for up to 120 iterations with a learning rate of 0.05. In the full
PSGSR pipeline, top-view and input-view refinement run for up to 100 iterations at 512 × 512 and
1024 × 1024 resolution, respectively. The learning rates for translation, yaw, and scale are 0.03,
0.03, and 0.02, respectively.

G.1
USDZ PACKAGE EXPORT AND CONFIGURATION
After scene registration and assembly, we convert each reconstructed scene into a self-contained
USDZ package for direct use in Isaac Sim. The assembled rigid-object scene, including the re-
constructed object poses, visual geometry, PBR materials, textures, and calibrated camera, is first
23


exported from Blender as a USD visual layer. Each GRAM-generated URDF is subsequently im-
ported into Isaac Sim as an independent articulation. Scene-level scale is baked into the correspond-
ing link meshes and joint frames, while the reconstructed articulation-root pose and observed joint
state are retained. The imported articulations are then composed with the rigid-object scene in a
shared metric, Z-up world frame.
The visual and collision representations are kept separate throughout export. Visual meshes preserve
their original geometry, materials, and textures. The supporting table uses its original unsimplified
triangle mesh as an exact static collider. Ordinary movable rigid objects use high-fidelity convex
decomposition with at most 128 convex hulls, at most 64 vertices per hull, a voxel resolution of 106,
a 1% approximation error, and shrink wrapping enabled.
For articulated objects, the collision meshes stored in the intermediate URDF are not used as the
final Isaac Sim collision representation. Instead, every articulated link receives an independent
signed-distance-field (SDF) collider generated from its original link geometry. The SDF resolution
and subgrid resolution are set to 128 and 6, respectively, using 16-bit subgrid values, a triangle-
reduction factor of 0.25, and no SDF remeshing. Compared with a single convex hull, this per-link
representation more faithfully preserves interaction-relevant concavities, including open containers,
internal compartments, and through-holes.
The final stage contains a PhysX scene with gravity set to 9.81 m/s2 along the negative Z-axis. Ar-
ticulation bases remain unfixed so that complete articulated objects can be moved through physical
interaction. Joint-drive stiffness and damping are both set to zero, allowing the joints to respond to
external operation instead of being locked by position drives. The reconstructed root poses, object
scales, observed joint positions, and intrinsic joint limits are authored directly into the USD stage.
All saved rigid-body linear velocities, angular velocities, and joint velocities are initialized to zero.
Dynamic collision geometries use a contact offset of 2 mm and a rest offset of 0.
Before packaging, all asset references are converted to paths relative to the root USD
layer.
Collision representations are constructed and checked in Isaac Sim, and the root
layer stores /physics/updateToUsd=true.
The root stage and all referenced USD
layers,

UsdUtils.CreateNewUsdzPackage. The resulting isaac asset package.usdz can
therefore be transferred and opened without access to the original reconstruction directory. Table 11

G.2
DIRECT-PACKAGE AND INITIAL-STATE VERIFICATION
To validate the released assets rather than intermediate pipeline states, we directly load each USDZ
package into Isaac Sim and render its initial state with the simulation timeline stopped. We verify
that the scene transforms and authored joint states remain unchanged during this zero-step rendering
and that the package is not modified. We then reopen the same package, initialize the PhysX runtime,
and restore its authored scene-entry state before applying gravity or external interaction. Thus,
the observed settling, rigid-body motion, and articulation are produced by simulation rather than
additional scene reconstruction or manual pose correction.
G.3
EDITING AND PHYSICS-BASED INTERACTION
The editing examples in the top row of Fig. 6 operate on independently addressable object instances.
Removal deletes a selected instance, insertion adds a newly generated asset, and rearrangement
changes existing instance transforms. Unchanged instances retain their reconstructed geometry and
articulation without rerunning GRAM or PSGSR.
For the gravity-settling test in the bottom row, selected objects are initialized slightly above their
target support regions before physics is enabled. Their subsequent settling is governed by gravity,
collision, and contact. For the transport test, the object inside the toolbox has no rigid attachment
or predefined parent–child connection to the toolbox; its motion during toolbox displacement arises
from contact with the interior collision geometry. After displacement, the lid is opened through
the recovered revolute joint while the contained object remains inside. This sequence tests support
contact, preservation of the internal cavity, and articulation with per-link collision geometry in the
assembled scene.
